# Unslop

Scan, rewrite, then reread and fix any remaining tells.

## Say what happened

- Preserve meaning, author voice, intended tone, and material uncertainty. Vary rhythm and structure. Use first person or a specific point of view where it fits. Keep real complexity visible.
- Name the actor and use active voice unless the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Name the mechanism, concrete action, or measured result. Cut vague impressions and sentences that could fit any project unchanged.
- Replace hype, inflated significance, generic challenges, and stock conclusions with facts or plans. Name the source and what it said. Cut empty name-dropping, vague attribution, and boilerplate about limited information.
- Cut trailing "-ing" clauses that add no fact. Supply evidence when the claim matters.
- Prefer plain words and strong verbs. Use "is", "has", and "use". Cut stock vocabulary such as "delve" or "pivotal", plus filler, weak adverbs, and stacked hedges. Give the number when it is known.
- Use one name per thing. Avoid forced threes, false ranges, and "not just X, but Y" framing.
- Write complete sentences with articles and verbs. Split sentences that need rereading. In prose, spell out arrows and abbreviations. Keep enough context to understand the action.
- Use literal terms. Avoid metaphors, aphorisms, personified code, and figurative verbs or nouns such as "wedge", "substrate", and "ratchet". Name the actual mechanism.

## Cut presentation tells

- Use periods or commas for breaks. Avoid em dashes and substitutes such as en dashes, spaced hyphens, and parentheses. Use colons before lists or examples only.
- Use sentence case, straight quotes, and restrained bolding. Drop decorative emojis and bold labels that repeat the line. A bold lead-in ending with a period can introduce new detail.
- Answer directly. Cut stock chatbot phrases, praise, and agreement openers.
